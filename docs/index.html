Local tasks / macOS

A menu-bar to-do app for macOS that plans your week.

Click the brain glyph in your menu bar, type a task, press Return. No Dock icon, no window, no account, no server, no network.

What's in the panel

Small, and finished.

Eight things the panel does. Pick one and the drawing beside it is the part of the panel that does it.

Instant capture

Click the icon, type, press Return. The field clears and stays focused, so you can add several in a row.

Start the text with #admin and it becomes an area instead of part of the task. The day picker aims the next capture somewhere other than today; the four buttons under it aim a quadrant.

Edit in place

Click a task's text and change it; edits save on Return or when you click away.

Clearing a row puts the old text back — deleting is the row's menu, never an empty field.

Drag to reorder or reschedule

Grab a task by its grip handle and drag it within a day, or onto another day's heading to move it there.

Dragging across quadrants retags the row to match where you dropped it — otherwise it would spring back the moment you let go. An area never changes that way: that one you type.

Done tallies

Each day heading shows what's left plus a green ✓ count of what you finished there this week. Click the tally to list those tasks, struck through, under the heading.

The count is credited to the task's day, not the day you ticked it, so the number matches the rows that vanished from that heading. It resets when the week does.

Delete, properly

Right-click a row and delete it. A task captured by mistake isn't done, and marking it done would inflate the tally you're meant to trust.

Deleting credits no tally and writes no archive entry. Both delete and complete give you about four seconds to undo.

Search

Press ⌘F and the week is replaced by the matches. Space-separated terms all have to match, in any order, across the text and the #area.

Word order is the thing nobody remembers, so “bank call” finds “Call the bank about the invoice”. The field isn't there until ⌘F asks for it, and it goes away with the panel.

Collapsible days

Click a day heading to fold it shut; the choice is remembered per space.

The rows go, the counts stay — so a shut day still says how much is in it. A task dragged onto a folded day unfolds it, so you see it arrive.

“Plan the day”

A distinct orange task appears once each morning at the top of today, in the Work space. Tick it, edit it or delete it and it stays gone until tomorrow.

It is pinned above every quadrant rather than sorted with them, and its box runs 10 minutes instead of 20. The Personal space is never handed a chore.

Why another to-do app

Four ordinary ideas, one click each.

Most to-do apps are a flat list, which is fine until the list is forty items long and every one of them looks equally urgent. Zundra puts four ordinary ideas about getting things done into one small panel, and makes them cost one click each.

The week, not the day

The list is split Monday→Sunday, so a task has a day rather than a vague someday. Anything that genuinely isn't for this week goes in a separate long-term bucket instead of rotting at the bottom.

The Eisenhower matrix, as one character

Each task carries an optional ! (do now), ↑ (schedule), → (delegate) or · (drop). Click to cycle it. The badge also orders the day, so triaging a list rearranges it.

Your own areas

Type #admin at the start of a task and it becomes a coloured capsule instead of part of the text. Areas are your words, made by using them — no list to set up, none to maintain, and each one keeps a distinct colour for good.

Timeboxing

Press ▶ on a task for a 20-minute focus box, or ✨ for a 60-minute box for something you've handed off and are waiting on. Several can run at once; the one finishing first shows next to the menu-bar icon.

Personal / Work

Two weeks, side by side.

Two spaces keep two weeks apart, each with its own days, tallies and fold state, so an evening's list never gets crowded out by a work one. The switch sits at the top of the panel, and everything below it belongs to the space on show.

Building it

There is no signed download yet.

Building takes a few seconds. You need macOS 13 (Ventura) or later and the Xcode Command Line Tools — xcode-select --install.

$ git clone https://github.com/mh-coder-1918/zundra.git
$ cd zundra
$ ./scripts/build-app.sh --install --run

That compiles the app, installs it to /Applications/Zundra.app and launches it. The brain icon appears in your menu bar.

What the flags do

  • no flags — build into ./build/Zundra.app only
  • --install — also copy it to /Applications
  • --install --run — also (re)launch it

The app is ad-hoc signed, which needs no Apple Developer account and is enough to run on your own Mac. The build fails rather than continuing if signing fails — a broken signature is worse than no build, because macOS reports it as damaged rather than merely unverified.

There is no Xcode project. The app is compiled straight from Sources/ by that script, so there is deliberately nothing to open in an IDE.

Your data

It stays in one folder on your Mac.

~/Library/Application Support/Zundra/
  tasks.json        the open tasks (one flat array)
  areas.json        each area's colour, assigned once and kept
  completed.json    this week's per-day ✓ counts
  archive.json      a record of completed tasks
  planning.json     when “Plan the day” was last seeded

If one of those files is ever unreadable, Zundra will not overwrite it. It moves the file aside under a dated name, starts without it, and says so in a strip at the top of the panel — so a corrupt byte costs you a restore, not your list.

Zundra asks for nothing and connects to nothing: no accounts, no analytics, no crash reporting, no network calls of any kind. The global shortcut uses a Carbon hotkey, so even that needs no Accessibility permission.

The name is from zadania — Polish for “tasks”. Apache-2.0 licensed; issues and pull requests are welcome.
